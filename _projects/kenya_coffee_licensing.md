Anyone who pulps, mills, warehouses, buys, sells, roasts, transports, imports or exports coffee in Kenya needs a valid licence, certificate or permit. Licences come from two **licensing authorities**: the **Coffee Directorate of the Agriculture and Food Authority (AFA)** and the **county government** where the business operates. Coffee brokers and the coffee exchange are licensed by the **Capital Markets Authority**. Growing coffee does not need a licence, but every grower must be **registered** with their county, and registration is free.

This guide is based on the [Crops (Coffee) (General) Regulations, 2019](/projects/kenya-coffee-laws.html) as amended to 2022. Fees and procedures can change, so confirm with the licensing authority before you apply.

## Licences issued by county governments

| Licence / certificate | What it allows | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| Grower registration | Every grower, cooperative society, association, estate and nursery operator registers with its county. The county issues the grower code and distinguishing mark. | Free |
| Coffee nursery certificate | Operate a coffee nursery (after a site inspection). | KSh 1,000 |
| Pulping station licence | Operate a pulping station (primary processing) and hull own parchment or buni. | KSh 1,000 |
| Grower miller licence | Pulp, mill, market or roast your own (or your members') coffee. | KSh 10,000 |
| Commercial coffee miller licence | Mill coffee as a business. Miller-marketers also need AFA's licence, and AFA must get the county's approval first. | US$500–2,000 by tonnes of clean coffee milled (under 3,000 t: US$500; 3,000–4,999 t: US$750; 5,000–9,999 t: US$1,000; over 10,000 t: US$2,000) |
| Coffee roaster licence | Buy clean coffee, roast or grind and package it for local sale. | KSh 2,500–10,000 by bags roasted (under 100 bags: KSh 2,500; 100–499: KSh 5,000; 500–999: KSh 7,500; over 1,000: KSh 10,000) |
| Warehouse licence | Operate a premises for storing coffee. It must meet KEBS standards and be certified by a public health officer. | US$500–1,000 by capacity |
| Movement permit (parchment or buni) | Move parchment or buni from the county of origin. | Free |
| Movement permit (clean coffee, from mill to warehouse) | Move clean coffee from the mill. | KSh 100 per leaf |

## Licences issued by AFA (Coffee Directorate)

| Licence / permit | What it allows | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee buyer's licence | Buy clean coffee at the auction for export, local sale or value addition (incorporated companies only). | US$100–500 by annual purchases (under 1,000 bags: US$100; 1,000–4,999: US$250; 5,000–9,999: US$350; over 10,000: US$500) |
| Miller-marketer licence | Mill and market growers' coffee as appointed by the grower. Commission is capped at 1% of sales value. | See commercial miller fees |
| Grower marketer licence | A grower marketing its own clean coffee. | Not specified in the Regulations |
| Warehouseman's licence | Offer warehouse management services (needs 3 years' experience). | KSh 2,000 |
| Independent cupping laboratory | Offer coffee liquoring (quality analysis) and training for a fee. | KSh 20,000 |
| Liquorer's practising licence | Work as a certified coffee liquorer (cupper). | KSh 2,000 |
| Auction organiser licence | Organise coffee auctions. | Not specified in the Regulations |
| Coffee import permit | Import processed (value-added) coffee. | KSh 10,000 |
| Movement permit (clean coffee bought at auction, export or import) | Move purchased clean coffee or ship it. | US$1 per leaf |

A **late application** costs an extra 10% of the licence fee. Certification schemes operating in Kenya must also register with AFA.

## Licensed by the Capital Markets Authority

- **Coffee exchange**: must be a demutualised company with enough capital for three years of operations.
- **Coffee broker**: a company limited by shares, with a chief executive who has at least five years' experience in coffee, commodities or securities. The applicant needs a letter from the exchange confirming it would be admitted. Apply to the [Capital Markets Authority](https://www.cma.or.ke/).

## Typical requirements

Requirements depend on the licence. Most applications need:

- the application form and a signed **self-declaration** (of debts, conflicts and litigation in the coffee industry, for millers, roasters and importers)
- national ID or passport details of the applicant, directors or officials
- for companies and societies: a certificate of incorporation or registration
- a **KRA tax compliance certificate**, and where relevant **NEMA**, **occupational safety and health**, and **public health** certificates
- for millers: comprehensive insurance, a licensed liquorer or cupping lab, and an "Authority to Erect" (or lease) for the mill
- for pulping stations: proof of land ownership or a lease of at least six years, a reliable water source, and a letter of no objection from the mother society where applicable
- for buyers: proof of engaging a licensed liquorer and access to a cupping lab, and a clearance letter from the exchange when renewing

## How licensing works

1. **Apply** to AFA or the county government on the prescribed form and pay the fee.
2. **Inspection and evaluation.** The licensing authority may inspect your premises.
3. **Public notice.** For new licences (other than growers' licences and certificates), the authority publishes a notice in the Kenya Gazette at least 30 days before granting the licence. Objections can be lodged within 14 days.
4. **Decision.** If your application is rejected, you must be told why within 14 days. You can fix the issues and apply again.
5. **Appeal.** If it is rejected again, you can appeal within 30 days: to the Cabinet Secretary for AFA licences, or to the County Executive Committee (through the County Secretary) for county licences. After that, you can go to court within 30 days.
6. **Keep it up.** Licences are not transferable. Holders must display them on request, file **monthly returns**, follow the Kenya Coffee Industry Standards and Code of Practice, and renew every year.

**Renewal dates:** the amended Regulations set the licence year as 1 October to 30 September and ask for renewals by 1 September. Another provision still refers to expiry on 30 June with renewal by 1 June. Check the current deadline with your licensing authority.

## Who to contact

- **AFA Coffee Directorate**: [afa.go.ke](https://afa.go.ke/)
- **Your county government**: the county department responsible for agriculture or cooperatives
- **Capital Markets Authority** (exchange and brokers): [cma.or.ke](https://www.cma.or.ke/)
