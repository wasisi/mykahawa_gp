Under the **[Coffee Act, 2026](/projects/kenya-coffee-laws.html)** (in force since 27 March 2026), coffee licences come from three **licensing authorities**:

- the **Coffee Board of Kenya**, which took over from the Agriculture and Food Authority (AFA)
- the **county government** where the business operates
- the **Capital Markets Authority (CMA)**, for the coffee exchange and brokers

Growing coffee needs **registration** with your county, not a licence, and registration is free. **Licences issued by AFA that were valid on 27 March 2026 remain valid** under the new Act.

## Who issues which licence

This is the list in the Second Schedule of the Coffee Act, 2026.

| Licence, permit or certificate | Issued by | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee buyer's licence | Coffee Board | Buying clean coffee at an exchange for export, local sale, value addition or secondary processing |
| Commercial coffee miller's licence | Coffee Board (operations also need a county licence) | Milling coffee for others: hulling and grading |
| Warehouse operator's licence | Coffee Board | Running or managing a coffee warehouse for reward |
| Independent cupping laboratory licence | Coffee Board | Coffee quality analysis services |
| Coffee liquorer's licence | Coffee Board | Practising as a certified liquorer (cupper) |
| Coffee trading licence | Coffee Board | Trading in the international coffee market and auction (growers or buyers) |
| Processed coffee import permit | Coffee Board | Importing processed coffee |
| Clean coffee movement permit for exports | Coffee Board | Moving clean coffee bought at auction, or for export or import |
| Warehouse licence | County government | Premises used to store coffee (must meet KEBS standards) |
| Pulping station licence | County government, with the Board | Operating a pulping station (factory) and hulling |
| Grower's milling licence | County government | A grower milling its own or its members' coffee |
| Coffee roaster's licence | County government, with the Board | Roasting coffee |
| Parchment permit from the county of origin | County government | Moving buni and parchment out of the county |
| Clean coffee movement permit from the county of origin | County government | Moving clean coffee from the mill to a warehouse |
| Coffee nursery certificate | Coffee Research and Training Institute | Operating a coffee nursery |
| Coffee exchange licence | Capital Markets Authority | Running a coffee exchange |
| Broker's licence | Capital Markets Authority | Offering growers' coffee for sale at an exchange |

The Board also registers **agents**, whom growers may appoint to handle logistics for overseas direct sales, and importers of coffee planting material. Certification schemes are registered with the Board too.

## Registration

Every coffee grower, nursery operator, grower miller, commercial miller, roaster, cooperative society, association and estate must **register with its county government**. Registration is free. A smallholder who is a member of a cooperative or association registered in the same county does not need to register separately. Instead, the cooperative submits its member register to the county at the start of each year and reports changes within 30 days. Counties share their registers with the Coffee Board every year.

## How to apply

1. **Apply** to the right licensing authority on the prescribed form, with the required information and fee.
2. **Decision within 14 days** (or a shorter period set by that authority's rules). A refusal must give reasons.
3. **Conditions.** Licences are **not transferable**. Holders must act in growers' best interests, follow their licence conditions, and file **quarterly returns**.
4. **Separation of roles.** A buyer (or an entity associated with it) cannot also hold a commercial miller, broker, roaster, agent or warehouse operator licence. A commercial miller cannot also be a buyer, broker, roaster or agent. A buyer's licence does not allow direct sales.
5. **Renewal.** All licences, certificates and permits **expire on 30 June** each year. Apply to renew by **1 June**. Late applications pay a late fee.
6. **Cancellation.** A licence can be cancelled if the holder breaks its conditions or the law, or surrenders it.
7. **Appeals.** If you disagree with a decision to refuse, revoke or cancel a licence, apply to the licensing authority's **Dispute Resolution Committee**, which decides within 30 days of the hearing. You can then appeal to the **High Court**.

Operating without the required licence is a serious offence. Buyers, importers, labs, liquorers, agents and warehouse operators face up to **KSh 5 million and/or 3 years** in prison. See [penalties](/projects/kenya-coffee-laws.html#disputes-and-penalties).

## Fees and requirements

The Coffee Act leaves fees, forms and detailed requirements to regulations made by the Cabinet Secretary (section 99) and, for county licences, to **county legislation** (sections 23–24). Until new rules are published, ask the Coffee Board or your county for current fees.

For reference, the last national fees, under the Crops (Coffee) (General) Regulations, 2019, were:

| Licence | Fee under the 2019 Regulations |
|---|---|
| Coffee buyer | US$100–500 a year, by annual purchases (under 1,000 bags: US$100; over 10,000 bags: US$500) |
| Commercial miller | US$500–2,000, by tonnes of clean coffee milled |
| Roaster | KSh 2,500–10,000, by bags roasted |
| Warehouse | US$500–1,000, by capacity |
| Grower's milling licence | KSh 10,000 |
| Pulping station licence; nursery certificate | KSh 1,000 each |
| Independent cupping lab | KSh 20,000 |
| Liquorer; warehouseman | KSh 2,000 each |
| Processed coffee import permit | KSh 10,000 |
| Late application | +10% of the licence fee |

Typical documents under the 2019 rules included: ID or passport details of the applicant and directors, a certificate of incorporation or registration, a KRA tax compliance certificate, and where relevant NEMA, occupational safety and health, and public health certificates. Millers also needed insurance, a licensed liquorer or cupping lab, and approval to build or lease a mill. Pulping stations needed proof of land ownership or a lease of at least six years and a reliable water source.

## Who to contact

- **Coffee Board of Kenya**: buyers, millers, warehouse operators, labs, liquorers, imports and exports. The Board took over AFA's coffee staff and offices. Look for transition notices on [afa.go.ke](https://afa.go.ke/) until the Board's own channels are published.
- **Your county government**: registration, pulping stations, growers' mills, roasters, warehouses and movement permits. Contact the county department for agriculture or cooperatives.
- **Capital Markets Authority**: exchange and brokers, at [cma.or.ke](https://www.cma.or.ke/).

Find licensed businesses in the [MyKahawa directory](/directory.html): [exporters and dealers](/directory/coffee-exporters.html), [millers](/directory/coffee-millers.html), [marketing agents](/directory/marketing-agents.html), [warehousemen](/directory/warehousemen.html) and [direct sales facilitators](/directory/direct-sales.html). Proposed licences are published in the Kenya Gazette with 14 days for objections, and the directory lists them as **Proposed**.
